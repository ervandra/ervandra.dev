Every business has technical debt. The question isn't whether you have it — it's whether you're managing it or letting it manage you.

## What Technical Debt Actually Looks Like

Technical debt isn't abstract. It shows up as:

- **Slow feature delivery.** What should take a week takes a month because the codebase is tangled.
- **Frequent outages.** Systems break because they were built for 100 users, not 10,000.
- **Developer turnover.** Good engineers leave when they're forced to maintain unmaintainable code.
- **Security vulnerabilities.** Outdated dependencies and patched-over fixes create attack surfaces.
- **Integration failures.** New tools can't connect to old systems without expensive custom work.

## The Compounding Problem

Technical debt works exactly like financial debt — it compounds. Every shortcut today becomes a constraint tomorrow. And unlike financial debt, there's no clear interest rate to warn you.

Here's how it compounds:

1. **Month 1-6:** Small workarounds. Developers know where the problems are. Everything still ships.
2. **Month 6-12:** New features take longer. Bug fixes create new bugs. Onboarding new developers is painful.
3. **Year 1-2:** Major features become impossible without a rewrite. Security concerns grow. Customers notice the slowdown.
4. **Year 2+:** The system is actively holding the business back. A rewrite becomes inevitable — and expensive.

## Measuring the Cost

You can approximate technical debt cost by looking at:

- **Velocity decline:** Are features taking longer to ship than 6 months ago?
- **Bug rate:** Is the ratio of bugs to features increasing?
- **Onboarding time:** How long until a new developer is productive?
- **Incident frequency:** Are outages becoming more common?

If you're seeing degradation in any of these metrics, you're paying interest on technical debt.

## The Pragmatic Approach

You don't need to eliminate all technical debt. You need to manage it strategically:

**1. Audit first.** Understand where the debt is and how it impacts the business.

**2. Prioritize by business impact.** Fix the debt that's blocking revenue or causing customer pain first.

**3. Budget for maintenance.** Allocate 15-20% of engineering time to debt reduction. This isn't overhead — it's investment.

**4. Prevent new debt.** Establish code review standards, architecture guidelines, and automated testing.

**5. Get technical leadership.** A fractional CTO can assess your debt objectively and create a realistic paydown plan.

## The Bottom Line

The cheapest time to address technical debt is now. The most expensive time is when it forces an emergency rewrite during your growth phase.
